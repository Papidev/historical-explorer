# Forum Boarium (forum-boarium)

The **Forum Boarium** was the ancient cattle market of **Rome**, situated on flat land near the **Tiber River** between the **Capitoline**, **Palatine**, and **Aventine hills**. As the site of **Rome**'s original docks, known as **Portus Tiberinus**, and adjacent to the **Pons Aemilius**, the city's earliest stone bridge, this area was a hub of intense commercial activity.

### History

This site was a significant religious center. It housed the **Temple of Hercules Victor**, the **Temple of Portunus** (also known as the **Temple of Fortuna Virilis**), and the large **Ara Maxima**, dating to the **6th or 5th century BC**. According to legend, the hero **Hercules** was honored here as a god by the ancient dwellers of the **Palatine hill** after he slew the giant **Cacus**, who had stolen **Geryon’s oxen**. The tufa stone core of an altar dedicated to **Hercules** is now inside the nearby church of **Santa Maria in Cosmedin**.

The **Forum Boarium** was also the location of **Rome**'s first gladiatorial contest, which took place in **264 BC**. This event was a funeral gift for the deceased father of **Marcus** and **Decimus Junius Brutus Scaeva**, who arranged the combat with three pairs of gladiators.

In **215 BC**, a grim event occurred here: four victims—a Gaulish man and woman, and a Greek man and woman—were buried alive. This human sacrifice was performed to appease the gods after a series of events were interpreted as omens of disaster. The Roman historian **Livy**, writing about **200 years later** in his **History of Rome**, described this practice as "most repulsive to Roman feelings." After the gods were believed to be propitiated, **Marcus Claudius Marcellus** sent troops to garrison **Rome**.

### Key Structures

The **Temple of Hercules Victor**, sometimes called **Hercules Olivarius**, is a circular building from the **2nd century BC**. It is **Rome**'s earliest surviving marble building, featuring a colonnade of Corinthian columns arranged around a cylindrical inner chamber, or cella, resting on a tuff foundation. For centuries, it was mistakenly known as the **Temple of Vesta**.

The **Temple of Portunus** is a rectangular structure built between **100** and **80 BC**. It has a portico with four free-standing Ionic columns at the front and engaged columns along its sides and rear, all mounted on a podium accessed by steps. This temple, constructed from tuff and travertine with a stucco finish, was long known as the **Temple of Fortuna Virilis**.

Historical sources suggest the **Forum** once displayed a statue by the sculptor **Myron**, looted from **Aegina**. While some accounts mention a cow, it might have been a group depicting **Theseus** defeating the **Minotaur**, a fitting subject for a cattle market.

During the late **Western Roman Empire**, the area transformed into a commercial district with many shops. Both temples were later converted into Christian churches. The nearby church of **Santa Maria in Cosmedin** is also home to the famous **Bocca della Verità**.

### Restoration

Beginning in the late **1990s**, a partnership between the **Soprintendenza speciale per i beni archeologici di Roma** and the **World Monuments Fund** led to the conservation of both temples within the **Forum Boarium**. The project also included new landscaping for the site. The **Arch of Janus** in the vicinity, however, remains unrestored.
